Chapter One

Today's web runs through a handful of front doors

Every post, payment and password you have online sits on someone else's server — a handful of companies you've never met, holding the master copy of your digital life.

That's convenient, until it isn't: one outage, one policy change, one breach, and everything routed through that single point goes down with it.

Web3 starts with a simple question: what if the ledger didn't have an owner at all?

02
Chapter Two

Blockchain rebuilds trust without a center

Instead of one server holding the truth, a blockchain keeps thousands of identical copies — one on every participating computer, called a node.

Each new block of transactions is cryptographically stitched to the one before it. Change a single record, and every link after it breaks — which is exactly why the chain is so hard to fake.

No CEO, no admin panel. Just math and a network that agrees.

Chapter Three

Six ideas built on top of that ledger

§

Cryptocurrency

Digital money that moves peer-to-peer, secured by cryptography instead of a bank in the middle.

§

Smart Contracts

Code that runs itself — "if X happens, do Y" — enforced by the network, not a middleman.

§

NFTs

A verifiable, on-chain certificate of ownership over a unique digital or real-world asset.

§

DAOs

Organizations run by token-holder votes and public code, instead of a CEO and a boardroom.

§

Wallets

Your identity and keys on-chain — no username or password, just a keypair only you hold.

§

Gas Fees

The small cost of asking the network to do work — payment for the computation, not a company.
